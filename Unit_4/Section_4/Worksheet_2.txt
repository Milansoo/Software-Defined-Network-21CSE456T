### Case Study – 2 (SLO-2): Applying Quantum Computing for Secure and Intelligent SDN Control

### a) Description of the SDN Architecture Integrated with Quantum Computing

The architecture connects national research assets (laboratories, supercomputing centers, and universities) using a multi-plane design that integrates quantum hardware and Quantum Key Distribution (QKD) alongside classical SDN infrastructure:

* **1. Dual-Layer Data & Quantum Forwarding Plane (Infrastructure Layer):**
* **Classical Optical/Packet Network:** Standard SDN-enabled optical transport nodes (ROADMs) and high-speed packet switches (P4/OpenFlow) that carry encrypted research payloads.
* **Quantum Channel Infrastructure (QKD Layer):** Dedicated dark fiber or wavelength-division multiplexed (WDM) fiber carrying single-photon quantum channels between research sites.
* **QKD Endpoints & Key Management Systems (KMS):** Physical QKD devices (transmitters/receivers) at each node that generate and store shared secret quantum keys (using protocols like BB84).


* **2. Quantum-Augmented SDN Control Plane:**
* **Centralized SDN Controller:** Manages classical routing, topology discovery, and flow rules via standard southbound protocols (OpenFlow, P4Runtime, NETCONF).
* **QKD Network Controller (QKDNC):** An orchestration agent (often standardized under ETSI GS QKD interfaces) integrated with the SDN controller to monitor quantum key pool levels, key consumption rates, and quantum bit error rates (QBER).
* **Quantum Co-Processing Engine (QC / Quantum Annealer):** A specialized quantum computing interface accessed via cloud or high-performance interconnects (e.g., using algorithms like QAOA or QUBO) to offload complex routing graph optimization and multi-tenant resource scheduling from the classical controller CPU.


* **3. Application & Management Plane:**
* **Secure Scientific Workflow Apps:** High-security NetApps handling data transfers (e.g., genomic data, high-energy physics models) that request quantum-secured circuits.
* **Zero Trust Policy & Key Orchestration:** Interacts with the controller via Northbound APIs to enforce dynamic cryptographic policies (e.g., renegotiating encryption keys every gigabyte or per bulk file transfer).

### b) How Quantum Computing Improves Security and Intelligence in SDN

* **Enhancing Network Security:**
* **Unconditional Forward Secrecy via QKD:**
* Classical key exchanges (like RSA or Diffie-Hellman) rely on mathematical difficulty and are vulnerable to future quantum decryption (Shor's Algorithm).
* QKD relies on the fundamental laws of quantum physics (Heisenberg’s Uncertainty Principle and the No-Cloning Theorem). The keys distributed between universities and labs cannot be copied, intercepted, or decrypted later by any quantum computer.


* **Tamper-Evident Channel Eavesdropping:**
* If an adversary attempts to tap or eavesdrop on the fiber carrying quantum states, the quantum wave function collapses, spiking the Quantum Bit Error Rate (QBER).
* The QKD controller immediately flags the security breach to the SDN controller, which isolates the compromised optical link in milliseconds and reroutes encrypted data over clean fiber.


* **Dynamic Encryption Key Provisioning for SDN Slices:**
* The SDN controller automates key distribution: when two laboratories establish an ad-hoc high-speed data flow, the controller provisions a flow rule that pairs the transmission with symmetric one-time pad (OTP) or AES-256 keys fetched directly from the local KMS.




* **Enhancing Network Intelligence:**
* **Ultra-Fast Global Optimization for Complex Topologies:**
* Finding optimal, multi-constrained paths (satisfying bandwidth, latency, jitter, and key-generation availability simultaneously) across national research meshes is an NP-hard problem.
* Quantum algorithms (such as QAOA or quantum annealing) evaluate massive combinatorial path configurations simultaneously through superposition, finding mathematically optimal global routes in milliseconds where classical heuristics produce sub-optimal delays.


* **Real-Time Traffic Rerouting and Self-Healing:**
* Research networks frequently experience sudden, multi-terabit bursts ("elephant flows") during experiments.
* A quantum-augmented SDN engine calculates global network traffic shifts concurrently, preventing link congestion and rebalancing national trunk links before packet drops occur.


* **Quantum Machine Learning (QML) for Threat Detection:**
* QML models can analyze multi-dimensional telemetry streams across thousands of edge ports in parallel, detecting stealthy, distributed attack signatures and reconnaissance scans far faster than classical pattern-matching firewalls.

SDN Worksheet Unit 4 Section 1
### Case Study – 1 (SLO-1): Understanding Data Center Challenges and the Need for SDN

---

### a) Key Challenges Faced by the Traditional Data Center

* **Manual, Device-by-Device Configuration:**
* Network administrators must log into individual switches and routers one at a time via CLI to make changes.
* This process is slow, labor-intensive, and prone to human errors, which leads to misconfigurations during critical peak periods.


* **Slow Deployment of New Services:**
* Provisioning new network resources or expanding e-commerce application capacity requires manual VLAN setup, ACL updates, and routing reconfigurations across multiple physical boxes.
* This delays launching new sales campaigns, microservices, or temporary infrastructure.


* **Frequent Network Congestion and Inefficient Bandwidth Utilization:**
* Traditional routing protocols typically pick the single shortest path (or rely on static Spanning Tree Protocol to block redundant links).
* This causes specific links to choke under heavy seasonal traffic while alternate physical links sit idle or underutilized.


* **Lack of Global Visibility and Centralized Control:**
* Control logic is distributed across every device. No single component has a complete, real-time map of all data center traffic flows.
* Administrators cannot see end-to-end paths, making traffic steering difficult and reactive rather than predictive.


* **Complex and Slow Troubleshooting:**
* Diagnosing bottlenecks or outages requires manually running diagnostics across dozens of individual network devices.
* Tracking down packet drops or latency spikes during seasonal sale peaks takes too long, increasing the risk of downtime and lost revenue.



---

### b) Why Software-Defined Networking (SDN) Is Suitable for Addressing These Challenges

* **Centralized Management and Global View:**
* SDN moves the decision-making brain (the control plane) into a central SDN controller.
* The controller maintains a complete, real-time view of the entire data center network topology and link utilization.
* Administrators can monitor the whole infrastructure from a single dashboard, making troubleshooting and traffic tracking much faster and simpler.


* **Dynamic Traffic Engineering and Congestion Control:**
* With a global view of all paths, the controller can dynamically balance traffic across all available physical links.
* During sudden flash-sale traffic spikes, the controller detects congested links and reroutes flows over underutilized paths automatically in milliseconds, eliminating traffic bottlenecks.


* **Automated and Rapid Service Provisioning:**
* SDN separates hardware forwarding from software policy.
* Network applications and automation scripts use Northbound APIs to program the network on demand.
* New application servers, firewalls, and routing rules can be spun up and configured in minutes via software without touching physical hardware cables or per-switch CLIs.


* **Flow-Based Granular Control:**
* Protocols like OpenFlow allow the controller to match on specific packet attributes (e.g., checkout traffic vs. bulk image downloads) and apply custom policies.
* Critical transactions (such as customer payments) can be prioritized with dedicated quality-of-service (QoS) queues to guarantee low latency during peak hours.


* **Elimination of Human Configuration Errors:**
* Network policies are defined once at the application/controller layer and pushed down uniformly to switches.
* This replaces error-prone manual typing across dozens of boxes with consistent, automated policy enforcement.

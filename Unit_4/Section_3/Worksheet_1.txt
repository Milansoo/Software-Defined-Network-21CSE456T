### Case Study – 1 (SLO-1): Understanding Network Slicing Concept and Challenges

---

### a) Concept of Network Slicing

* **What It Is:**
* Network slicing is a technique that carves up a single, shared physical network into multiple independent, dedicated virtual networks called **"slices."**
* Instead of building three separate physical networks for video, vehicles, and sensors, the operator runs all three virtually over the same towers, fiber cables, and servers.


* **End-to-End Separation:**
* Slicing is not just a software tag on a router; it extends across every segment of the telecom infrastructure:
* **Radio Access Network (RAN):** Splitting wireless frequencies and antenna time slots among devices.
* **Transport Network:** Reserving dedicated bandwidth and latency bounds over optical fiber or IP routers.
* **Core Network & Edge Cloud:** Spinning up dedicated virtualized network functions (VNFs/containers) for data routing and user authentication.




* **How It Serves the Three Diverse Use Cases:**
* **eMBB (High-Speed Video Streaming):**
* Configured for **maximum bandwidth and bulk throughput**.
* Routes traffic to high-capacity central data centers with large packet buffers to ensure continuous high-definition streaming.


* **URLLC (Autonomous Vehicles):**
* Configured for **sub-millisecond latency and ultra-high reliability (99.999%)**.
* Terminates traffic at edge servers (Multi-Access Edge Computing / MEC) close to roadside towers to avoid long-distance routing delays, with preemptive radio scheduling so vehicle safety packets never wait in line.


* **mMTC (Massive IoT Sensors):**
* Configured for **high device connection density and low energy consumption**.
* Strips away unnecessary voice/mobility signaling so battery-powered smart meters can send small, occasional data packets and sleep for months without draining batteries.





---

### b) Key Challenges in Implementing Network Slicing

* **Ensuring Strict Isolation (Performance and Security):**
* **Performance Isolation:** Preventing "noisy neighbor" issues. For instance, a sudden surge of mobile users streaming 4K video on the eMBB slice must not consume shared compute or bandwidth needed by the safety-critical URLLC autonomous vehicle slice.
* **Security Isolation:** Containing faults or breaches inside a single slice. If an insecure IoT sensor on the mMTC slice is compromised, the operator must guarantee the attacker cannot perform lateral movement or side-channel attacks across the shared hypervisors into the automotive control slice.


* **Dynamic End-to-End Orchestration Complexity:**
* Coordinating and stitching resources across three different domains (RAN, Transport, and Core) simultaneously is technically challenging.
* The orchestrator must automatically provision, scale up, scale down, and decommission slices in real time based on changing traffic demand without manual intervention.


* **RAN-Level Resource Slicing:**
* While slicing compute servers using virtual machines or containers is straightforward, slicing the shared wireless radio spectrum is physically constrained.
* Dividing radio channels dynamically among high-bandwidth video, instantaneous vehicle control, and millions of sporadic IoT pings requires complex radio schedulers and advanced beamforming algorithms.


* **SLA Monitoring and Real-Time Assurance:**
* Different enterprise customers have legally binding Service Level Agreements (SLAs).
* The operator requires real-time telemetry to continuously monitor metrics (packet loss, latency jitter, throughput) on each slice and trigger automatic self-healing or re-routing before an SLA threshold is violated.


* **Legacy Infrastructure Compatibility:**
* True end-to-end network slicing requires a 5G Standalone (5G SA) cloud-native core with modern SDN and NFV capabilities.
* Migrating from legacy, proprietary 4G/LTE hardware boxes to fully virtualized, open infrastructure requires substantial capital investment and complex architectural transitions.

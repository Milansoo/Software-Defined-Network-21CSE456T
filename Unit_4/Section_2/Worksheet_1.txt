### Case Study Question (SLO-1): 6G Network Security Challenges

---

### a) Major Network Security Challenges Specific to 6G Networks

* **Vulnerabilities in AI-Native Control:**
* 6G builds artificial intelligence directly into the network core and radio management rather than using AI as an add-on.
* Attackers can launch **adversarial AI attacks**, such as data poisoning (feeding bad data to train network models) or evasion attacks, tricking the automated controller into misrouting traffic, dropping connections, or opening backdoors.


* **Physical and Directional Risks in Terahertz (THz) Frequencies:**
* THz signals use ultra-narrow, directional beamforming and intelligent reflecting surfaces (IRS/RIS) to overcome short range and high signal loss.
* Attackers can deploy rogue reflective surfaces to manipulate or hijack beams, or physically align eavesdropping sensors directly along the pencil-thin beam path to intercept data.


* **Massive Expansion of the Attack Surface:**
* Connecting billions of micro-sensors, smart dust, autonomous vehicles, and holographic endpoints drastically increases the number of potential entry points.
* Many tiny IoT nodes have limited processing power and battery life, preventing them from running heavy security algorithms and making them prime targets for botnets.


* **Threats to Edge and Distributed Computing:**
* To hit sub-millisecond latency targets, compute power moves to micro-edge nodes scattered throughout the city (on lampposts, traffic lights, and bus stops).
* These small, physically accessible edge locations are far more vulnerable to direct physical tampering, unauthorized hardware taps, and local credential theft than locked, central data centers.


* **Post-Quantum Cryptography Risks:**
* 6G networks will operate alongside the emergence of practical quantum computers.
* Legacy public-key encryption schemes (like RSA and standard ECC) can be broken by quantum algorithms, exposing transmitted municipal telemetry and identity data to "harvest now, decrypt later" attacks.



---

### b) Why Traditional 4G/5G Security Mechanisms Are Insufficient for 6G

* **Static, Perimeter-Based Security Fails in a Decentralized Fabric:**
* 4G and 5G rely heavily on centralized authentication points (like the Core Network HSS/UDM) and fixed perimeter firewalls.
* 6G is fully decentralized, mesh-like, and dynamic; devices constantly connect directly to nearby edge nodes and each other (Device-to-Device / D2D), bypassing traditional central inspection checkpoints.


* **Encryption and Handshake Overhead Breaks Sub-Millisecond Latency:**
* Legacy authentication handshakes (such as 5G-AKA) and multi-roundtrip key exchanges take several milliseconds to complete.
* Mission-critical 6G tasks (like real-time collision avoidance for autonomous cars and tactile holographic interaction) require microsecond-level response times; standard heavy handshakes introduce unacceptable latency delays.


* **Rule-Based Firewalls Cannot Detect AI-Driven Exploits:**
* 4G/5G intrusion detection tools rely on static signatures and pre-programmed threshold rules.
* These tools cannot identify subtle, automated attacks that manipulate machine learning models (model poisoning, gradient leakage, or policy spoofing) within the 6G AI-native control loop.


* **Lack of Integrated Communication and Sensing (ISAC) Security:**
* 6G combines wireless data communication with radar-like spatial sensing on the same radio waves (tracking object locations, speeds, and shapes).
* 4G/5G security protocols are designed strictly for data transport; they provide zero protections against sensory eavesdropping, location spoofing, or unauthorized environmental scanning.


* **Centralized Certificate Authorities Cannot Scale to Trillions of Devices:**
* Traditional Public Key Infrastructure (PKI) models require centralized server lookups to verify identity certificates.
* In a 6G city with trillions of short-lived, transient IoT connections, central certificate validation servers would face severe connection bottlenecks, requiring automated, decentralized trust models (such as zero-trust distributed ledgers).
